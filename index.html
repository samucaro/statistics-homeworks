Statistics — Homeworks

MSc in Cybersecurity · Sapienza University of Rome · A.A. 2026/27

  • HW1 — From private key to Ethereum address
    Part 1: scalar multiplication on y² = x³ + 7 (mod 17), double-and-add, step-by-step visualisation, statistical properties of the outputs, cryptography & statistics in Bitcoin and Ethereum.
    Part 2: BIP-39, BIP-32, BIP-44, secp256k1, Keccak-256, EIP-55 — a live pipeline from mnemonic to checksummed address, statistical properties of each step, SHA-2 vs Keccak/SHA-3, historical context.
  • HW2 — Toy elliptic-curve encryption vs the Caesar cipher
    EC-ElGamal on secp256k1 with a restricted private key, the Caesar cipher, statistical cryptanalysis (χ², n-grams, unicity distance), brute-force and baby-step giant-step attacks, design flaws.
  • HW3 — Penetration scores as random walks: LLN and CLT
    n systems and m attackers with Bernoulli or Rademacher increments: total, average and normalised score, variance scaling, law of large numbers, central limit theorem (with exact Berry–Esseen check), aggregate risk.
  • HW4 — Welford's recurrences and the sampling mean and variance
    Ex. 1: derivation and 3-line code of Welford's recurrences, worked example, numerical stability.
    Ex. 2: m hackers × n systems with scores in [0, 10]: average sampling mean and variance vs μ and σ², bias, effect of n.
  • HW5 — Riemann vs Lebesgue integration, and the normal CDF
    Ex. 1: domain vs range partitions on regular, unbounded and pathological functions (Dirichlet, Thomae): accuracy, convergence, cost.
    Ex. 2: Φ via quadrature, erf series, tail continued fraction and a hybrid method at double precision; Liouville's theorem and the error function.
  • HW6 — Contingency tables, independence and the χ² test
    Contingency tables built from raw records (NSL-KDD intrusion data, Berkeley 1973 admissions, synthetic, your own CSV): marginal and conditional frequencies, expected counts, χ² and p-value, permutation test, Simpson's paradox, association vs causality.
  • HW7 — Modes of convergence, and Bayesian inference
    Part 1: almost sure, in probability, mean-square and in-distribution convergence with counterexamples; their role in the LLN, CLT and inference; why √n (additivity of variance, independence).
    Part 2: conditional probability, Bayes' theorem, prior–likelihood–posterior: IDS base-rate fallacy, phishing click rate (Beta–Binomial), sequential learning.
Each homework is a static page with client-side JavaScript; no data is collected.
